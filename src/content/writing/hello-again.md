So it has been a long time since I updated my website. Well, it has been a journey: six years. Recapping it will take some time, but I have a few paragraphs to share what I learned from various stints and the things I am grateful for. I will leave what's next for me for the next article. What is certain, though, is that an academic path feels like the only path that allows for the intellectual freedom I need at this stage.

Wadhwani AI was such a roller-coaster ride. I spent nearly two years working there, focusing on multiple projects. On one of the projects, I was part of a three-person team. I had so much fun working on it: high ownership and clearly scoped work made it much easier to make the project a success. I am glad to have met [Keshav](https://www.linkedin.com/in/keshav-ramaswami/) and [Dr. Rahul](https://www.linkedin.com/in/rahulap/) during this project. I couldn't have asked for a better first project at the start of my career. Wanting to focus on agriculture, I joined the [Cotton Ace](https://www.wadhwaniai.org/impact/agriculture-solutions/crop-ace/) project. I was part of a large ML team, where I focused on improving our deployed object-detection model by adding a pre-rejection model to reject photos for which we shouldn't make any predictions. This project introduced me to concepts such as model drift, in which a model becomes worse as more data comes in. The cause could be data, concept, or label drift—things that become important as soon as you increase the scale of deployment. Given the more mature nature of the second project, it was less exciting but equally rewarding. On both projects, however, I loved that I was able to see deployments in the field. You learn about things you would never consider if you didn't leave the lab. I am grateful to have met very kind people during my time there. I cherish the friendships I found with fellow associates such as [Nikhil](https://shenoynikhil.com), [Pulkit](https://madaanpulkit.github.io), [Anmol](https://www.linkedin.com/in/anmolojha15/), [Chandan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/chan4899/), and [Makkunda](https://makkunda.github.io). [Jigar](https://www.jigarkdoshi.com) and [Jerome](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jerome-white/) mentored our team, and I find them great at breaking down complexity. Lastly, during my final few months at Wadhwani, I saw how deeply a company's direction and culture are anchored in the people who hold executive roles. When Wadhwani went through an overhaul, with new people filling major executive positions, it felt as if I were in a new organization. I certainly realized this much later.

Next came Rephrase AI. I was amazed by the concentration of brainpower at the organization. I remember that whenever I was stuck on a training problem, my manager, [Nisheeth](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nisheethlahoti/), would look at the loss curve and predict that a certain function was preventing certain parameters from being learned. This was the first time I saw how an intuitive understanding of mathematics could help you see solutions that are far from obvious otherwise. It was nothing short of impressive how the team was able to achieve seemingly impossible things by thinking from first principles and because they really wanted to solve the problem of virtual avatars. I also realized that I had wanted to join Rephrase because I wanted to be closer to research. Being indifferent to the idea of virtual avatars made it harder for me to push. It taught me an important lesson: work on things you believe in and can imagine pursuing for years without becoming bored. Even after learning this, it took me some time to put curiosity in the driver's seat consistently.

Finally, I came to Tübingen to continue my studies because I never gave up on my long-standing dream of earning a master's degree. Since I couldn't do it in 2020 because of COVID-19, it was time to return to that dream. I was driven by both curiosity about the world outside India and, to some extent, a desire to return to research. Landing in Germany and immediately signing up for 150% of the suggested credits for my first semester wasn't smart. Once again, I wasn't giving curiosity space to guide me; instead, I was forcing myself to be "productive." Maybe the need to feel "productive" is a result of a scarcity mindset? I am not sure, but it certainly makes you choose things you would not choose if you did not view failure negatively. The last two and a half years have been intense. I have grown so much as a human that it feels strange not to have moved earlier. Throwing yourself into a different country allows you to see the customs of your home country for what they are: customs. Moving certainly teaches you so much about the world and yourself. Having moved to Europe has made me much more careful about my health. In India, it was always an afterthought, but here, not so much. I am fitter today than I was when I moved from India, and it shows in my blood reports from [Apoorv Pathology](https://www.apoorvpathology.com) whenever I get to visit home.

While in Tübingen, I also got to experiment with working in traditionally large companies. My experience at Mercedes was a mixed bag. Life is certainly more organized when working for a large company than at a startup, but one of the problems I faced was the language barrier. My German today is at the B1–B2 level. During my time at Mercedes, when my German was perhaps at the A2 level, I remember being in group meetings held in German. I would not understand the discussion, which effectively meant that I wasn't in the meeting. Lunch conversations would always happen in German. This experience was very disappointing, but there is no quick, logical solution: if team members are simply more comfortable in German, you can't switch that easily. This gave me an insight that I still hold: I wouldn't be able to work at a place where the lingua franca is not English. This rules out large organizations in Germany and elsewhere in Europe where the local language dominates the workplace.

After Mercedes, I wanted to explore participating in an incubator, and the Cyber Valley Incubator was the perfect opportunity.
